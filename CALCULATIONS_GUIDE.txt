1. WORKBENCH CALCULATIONS
================================================================================

These calculations occur when users create simulations and input marketing 
action data. They determine how individual marketing actions contribute to 
an overall Account Score.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 ACTION WEIGHT CALCULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Automatically distribute importance (weight) across all marketing actions 
based on their Proposed Scores. Actions with higher proposed scores get 
higher weights, and all weights must sum to exactly 100%.

FORMULA:
Weight (%) = (Proposed Score / Total of All Proposed Scores) × 100

WHERE VALUES COME FROM:
- Proposed Score: User input for each action (e.g., 8, 10, 15)
- Total Proposed Scores: Sum of all Proposed Scores for all actions

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:
Given these actions:
  Action              Proposed Score
  ----------------    --------------
  Email Opens         8
  Outbound Response   10
  Page Views          5
  Content Downloads   12
  Webinar Attendance  10
  Demo Requests       15
  ----------------    --------------
  TOTAL               60

Individual Weight Calculations:
  Email Opens:         (8 / 60) × 100  = 13.33%
  Outbound Response:   (10 / 60) × 100 = 16.67%
  Page Views:          (5 / 60) × 100  = 8.33%
  Content Downloads:   (12 / 60) × 100 = 20.00%
  Webinar Attendance:  (10 / 60) × 100 = 16.67%
  Demo Requests:       (15 / 60) × 100 = 25.00%
                                         -------
  TOTAL                                  100.00%

WHY THIS MATTERS:
This ensures that actions with higher engagement potential automatically 
receive proportionally more importance in the final Account Score calculation.

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → calculateActionWeight()


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 COMPLETE SCORE CALCULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Calculate the total engagement value for each marketing action by multiplying 
the quality (Proposed Score) by quantity (Events).

FORMULA:
Complete Score = Events × Proposed Score

WHERE VALUES COME FROM:
- Events: User input - number of times this action occurred (e.g., 50 emails)
- Proposed Score: User input - quality/value rating of the action (e.g., 8/10)

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:
Given:
  Email Opens:
    - Events = 50 (50 emails were opened)
    - Proposed Score = 8 (each open is worth 8 points)
    
  Complete Score = 50 × 8 = 400

REAL-WORLD INTERPRETATION:
If you sent emails that were opened 50 times, and each open is worth 8 points
of engagement quality, your total engagement value is 400 points.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
This combines both QUANTITY (how many times) and QUALITY (how valuable) to 
give a comprehensive engagement metric. 100 low-quality interactions might 
be worth less than 10 high-quality interactions.

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → calculateCompleteScore()


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3 ACCOUNT SCORE CALCULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Combine all marketing actions into a single, weighted score that represents 
the overall engagement level of an account. This is THE KEY METRIC used for 
cohort classification.

FORMULA:
Account Score = Σ[(Weight% / 100) × Complete Score] for all actions

WHERE VALUES COME FROM:
- Weight%: Calculated from section 1.1
- Complete Score: Calculated from section 1.2

STEP-BY-STEP EXAMPLE:
Given these calculated values:

Action              Weight%   Complete Score   Contribution
----------------    -------   --------------   ------------
Email Opens         13.33%    400              (0.1333 × 400) = 53.32
Outbound Response   16.67%    300              (0.1667 × 300) = 50.01
Page Views          8.33%     200              (0.0833 × 200) = 16.66
Content Downloads   20.00%    600              (0.2000 × 600) = 120.00
Webinar Attendance  16.67%    500              (0.1667 × 500) = 83.35
Demo Requests       25.00%    750              (0.2500 × 750) = 187.50
                                                              -------
ACCOUNT SCORE                                                 510.84

DETAILED BREAKDOWN:
1. Email Opens contributed:    53.32 points (10.4% of total)
2. Outbound Response:           50.01 points (9.8% of total)
3. Page Views:                  16.66 points (3.3% of total)
4. Content Downloads:          120.00 points (23.5% of total)
5. Webinar Attendance:          83.35 points (16.3% of total)
6. Demo Requests:              187.50 points (36.7% of total)

WHY THIS MATTERS:
The Account Score (510.84) tells us this is a HIGHLY ENGAGED account because:
- Score > 400, so it falls into Cohort 3 (high engagement)
- Demo Requests were the major contributor (36.7%)
- The account showed strong engagement across multiple channels

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → calculateAccountScore()


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4 MAJOR CONTRIBUTOR IDENTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Identify which single marketing action had the biggest impact on the Account 
Score. This helps marketers understand what's driving engagement.

FORMULA:
For each action:
  Contribution = (Weight% / 100) × Complete Score

Major Contributor = Action with highest Contribution value

EXAMPLE (using data from 1.3):
Comparing all contributions:
  - Email Opens:        53.32
  - Outbound Response:  50.01
  - Page Views:         16.66
  - Content Downloads: 120.00
  - Webinar Attendance: 83.35
  - Demo Requests:     187.50 ← HIGHEST

RESULT:
Major Contributor: Demo Requests (187.50 points)

INTERPRETATION:
"Demo Requests" was the primary driver of this account's engagement score,
contributing 187.50 out of 510.84 total points (36.7%).

WHY THIS MATTERS:
Understanding the major contributor helps you:
1. Double down on what's working
2. Allocate resources to high-impact channels
3. Personalize outreach based on engagement patterns

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → findMajorContributor()

2. COHORT CLASSIFICATION
================================================================================

After calculating the Account Score, we classify accounts into cohorts based
on their engagement level. This drives all subsequent revenue forecasting.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 ACCOUNT-TO-COHORT MAPPING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CLASSIFICATION RULES:
  Account Score Range    →    Cohort Assignment    →    Engagement Level
  -------------------         ------------------         ----------------
  100 - 199                   Cohort 1                   Low Engagement
  200 - 299                   Cohort 2                   Medium Engagement
  300 - 400                   Cohort 3                   High Engagement
  < 100                       Cohort 1 (default)         Very Low
  > 400                       Cohort 3 (default)         Very High

EXAMPLES:
  Account Score: 150  → Cohort 1 (within 100-199 range)
  Account Score: 250  → Cohort 2 (within 200-299 range)
  Account Score: 511  → Cohort 3 (>400, defaults to Cohort 3)
  Account Score: 85   → Cohort 1 (<100, defaults to Cohort 1)

WHY THESE RANGES:
The ranges create meaningful segments:
- Cohort 1: Showing some interest but low engagement
- Cohort 2: Moderate engagement across channels
- Cohort 3: Highly engaged, strong buying signals

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → classifyAccountIntoCohort()


================================================================================
3. COHORT METRICS CALCULATIONS
================================================================================

Once accounts are classified into cohorts, we calculate business metrics 
(Win Rate, Deal Size, etc.) for each cohort. These are based on industry 
benchmarks for B2B SaaS companies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 WIN RATE CALCULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Predict what percentage of opportunities in each cohort will close as deals.
Higher engagement = higher win rates.

FORMULA:
Win Rate = Base Rate + Volume Bonus (capped at 60%)

WHERE:
  Volume Bonus = min(Account Count × 0.05, 5)

BASE RATES (Industry Benchmarks):
  Cohort 1: 17.5%  (Low engagement → fewer wins)
  Cohort 2: 30.0%  (Medium engagement → moderate wins)
  Cohort 3: 45.0%  (High engagement → high wins)

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:

Scenario: Cohort 2 with 60 accounts

Step 1: Get base rate
  Base Rate = 30.0% (Cohort 2)

Step 2: Calculate volume bonus
  Volume Bonus = min(60 × 0.05, 5)
  Volume Bonus = min(3.0, 5)
  Volume Bonus = 3.0%

Step 3: Add bonus to base (with 60% cap)
  Win Rate = 30.0% + 3.0%
  Win Rate = 33.0%
  
  Check cap: 33.0% < 60%, so no capping needed
  Final Win Rate = 33.0%

INDUSTRY CONTEXT:
These rates are based on B2B SaaS benchmarks:
- Low engagement accounts: 15-20% win rate (cold leads)
- Medium engagement: 25-35% win rate (warm leads)
- High engagement: 40-50% win rate (hot leads)

The volume bonus reflects "economies of scale" - with more accounts, you
optimize your sales process and learn what works, slightly improving rates.

WHY THE 60% CAP:
Even with perfect engagement, not every opportunity closes. Market conditions,
competition, budget constraints, and timing all factor in. A 60% cap is 
realistic for even the best-performing cohorts.

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → calculateCohortWinRate()


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 AVERAGE DEAL SIZE CALCULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Predict the average dollar value of deals that close in each cohort.
Higher engagement typically correlates with larger deal sizes.

FORMULA:
Avg Deal Size = Base Deal Size × Volume Adjustment

WHERE:
  Volume Adjustment = 0.95 if Account Count > 100, else 1.0

BASE DEAL SIZES (Industry Benchmarks):
  Cohort 1: $50,000   (Small deals, limited scope)
  Cohort 2: $80,000   (Medium deals, growing accounts)
  Cohort 3: $125,000  (Enterprise deals, strategic accounts)

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:

Scenario A: Cohort 3 with 45 accounts

Step 1: Get base deal size
  Base = $125,000 (Cohort 3)

Step 2: Check account count
  45 accounts ≤ 100, so Volume Adjustment = 1.0

Step 3: Calculate final size
  Avg Deal Size = $125,000 × 1.0
  Avg Deal Size = $125,000

Scenario B: Cohort 1 with 120 accounts

Step 1: Get base deal size
  Base = $50,000 (Cohort 1)

Step 2: Check account count
  120 accounts > 100, so Volume Adjustment = 0.95

Step 3: Calculate final size
  Avg Deal Size = $50,000 × 0.95
  Avg Deal Size = $47,500

INDUSTRY CONTEXT:
B2B SaaS deal sizes by engagement:
- Low engagement: $40K-$60K (starter/basic plans)
- Medium engagement: $70K-$90K (professional plans)
- High engagement: $100K-$150K (enterprise contracts)

WHY THE VOLUME ADJUSTMENT:
With more accounts (>100), you typically have a mix of deal sizes. Some
smaller deals dilute the average slightly. This 5% reduction reflects that
reality without being overly pessimistic.

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → calculateCohortAvgDealSize()


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 SALES CYCLE CALCULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Predict how many days it takes to close a deal in each cohort.
Higher engagement = faster sales cycles.

FORMULA:
Sales Cycle = Base Cycle × Process Efficiency

WHERE:
  Process Efficiency = 0.9 if Account Count > 50, else 1.0

BASE CYCLES (Industry Benchmarks):
  Cohort 1: 135 days  (Long nurture period needed)
  Cohort 2: 90 days   (Moderate evaluation time)
  Cohort 3: 60 days   (Fast decision makers)

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:

Scenario A: Cohort 2 with 30 accounts

Step 1: Get base cycle
  Base = 90 days (Cohort 2)

Step 2: Check account count
  30 accounts ≤ 50, so Process Efficiency = 1.0

Step 3: Calculate final cycle
  Sales Cycle = 90 × 1.0
  Sales Cycle = 90 days

Scenario B: Cohort 3 with 75 accounts

Step 1: Get base cycle
  Base = 60 days (Cohort 3)

Step 2: Check account count
  75 accounts > 50, so Process Efficiency = 0.9

Step 3: Calculate final cycle
  Sales Cycle = 60 × 0.9
  Sales Cycle = 54 days (rounded)

INDUSTRY CONTEXT:
B2B SaaS sales cycles by engagement:
- Low engagement: 120-150 days (cold → warm → hot)
- Medium engagement: 80-100 days (already somewhat interested)
- High engagement: 50-70 days (ready to buy, just evaluating)

WHY THE PROCESS EFFICIENCY:
With more accounts (>50), your sales team:
1. Develops better playbooks
2. Recognizes patterns faster
3. Optimizes their pitch and demos
This leads to a ~10% reduction in cycle time.

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → calculateCohortSalesCycle()


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 FORECASTED MARKETING REVENUE ATTRIBUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Calculate how much revenue can be attributed to marketing efforts for each
cohort. This is THE KEY OUTPUT - it shows marketing's financial impact.

FORMULA:
Revenue = Accounts × (Win Rate / 100) × Avg Deal Size × Attribution Factor

WHERE:
  Attribution Factor represents marketing's influence on the deal

MARKETING ATTRIBUTION FACTORS:
  Cohort 1: 0.3 (30% - lower marketing influence)
  Cohort 2: 0.5 (50% - moderate marketing influence)
  Cohort 3: 0.7 (70% - high marketing influence)

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:

Scenario: Cohort 2 with these metrics:
  - Accounts: 60
  - Win Rate: 33% (from section 3.1)
  - Avg Deal Size: $80,000 (from section 3.2)
  - Attribution Factor: 0.5 (Cohort 2)

Step 1: Calculate expected wins
  Expected Wins = Accounts × (Win Rate / 100)
  Expected Wins = 60 × 0.33
  Expected Wins = 19.8 deals

Step 2: Calculate total revenue from those wins
  Total Revenue = Expected Wins × Avg Deal Size
  Total Revenue = 19.8 × $80,000
  Total Revenue = $1,584,000

Step 3: Apply marketing attribution
  Marketing Revenue = Total Revenue × Attribution Factor
  Marketing Revenue = $1,584,000 × 0.5
  Marketing Revenue = $792,000

INTERPRETATION:
Out of the 60 accounts in Cohort 2:
- We expect to close 19.8 deals (33% win rate)
- Those deals will generate $1,584,000 in total revenue
- Marketing can claim credit for $792,000 (50% attribution)
- The other $792,000 is credited to sales, product, brand, etc.

WHY ATTRIBUTION FACTORS VARY BY COHORT:

Cohort 1 (30% attribution):
  - Marketing generates awareness and initial interest
  - Sales does heavy lifting to nurture and close
  - Product quality and competitive pricing matter more
  - Marketing's role is important but not dominant

Cohort 2 (50% attribution):
  - Marketing significantly influences the buyer journey
  - Content, campaigns, and events move prospects forward
  - Balanced partnership between marketing and sales
  - Marketing deserves equal credit

Cohort 3 (70% attribution):
  - Highly engaged accounts are driven by marketing efforts
  - Content, webinars, demos created strong pull
  - Sales mostly facilitates rather than convinces
  - Marketing is the primary driver of these deals

INDUSTRY CONTEXT:
Marketing attribution in B2B SaaS typically ranges from 20-80% depending on:
- Sales cycle length (longer = more touchpoints = higher attribution)
- Product complexity (complex = more content = higher attribution)
- Market maturity (mature = more education needed = higher attribution)

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → calculateCohortMarketingRevenue()


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.5 YEAR-OVER-YEAR DELTA CALCULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PURPOSE:
Measure the percentage change in metrics from one fiscal year to the next.
This shows growth trends and helps with forecasting.

FORMULA:
Delta % = ((Current Year Value - Previous Year Value) / Previous Year Value) × 100

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:

Scenario: Cohort 2 Revenue
  - FY 24-25: $750,000
  - FY 25-26: $792,000

Step 1: Calculate the difference
  Difference = $792,000 - $750,000
  Difference = $42,000

Step 2: Calculate percentage change
  Delta % = ($42,000 / $750,000) × 100
  Delta % = 0.056 × 100
  Delta % = 5.6%

INTERPRETATION:
Cohort 2's marketing-attributed revenue grew by 5.6% year-over-year,
representing $42,000 in additional revenue.

SPECIAL CASE: Division by Zero
If Previous Year Value = 0:
  - If Current Year > 0: Delta = +100%
  - If Current Year = 0: Delta = 0%

This handles new cohorts or metrics that didn't exist in prior years.

CODE LOCATION: src/utils/cohortCalculations.ts → calculateForecastDelta()

4.1 COMPLETE WORKFLOW EXAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let's follow ONE account through the entire system:

  Email Opens:         (8/58) × 100  = 13.79%

STEP 3: Calculate Complete Scores
----------------------------------
  Email Opens:         45 × 8   = 360
  Outbound Response:   12 × 10  = 120
  Page Views:          150 × 3  = 450
  Content Downloads:   25 × 12  = 300
  Webinar Attendance:  8 × 10   = 80
  Demo Requests:       5 × 15   = 75

STEP 4: Calculate Account Score
--------------------------------
  Email Opens:         0.1379 × 360 = 49.64
  Outbound Response:   0.1724 × 120 = 20.69
  Page Views:          0.0517 × 450 = 23.27
  Content Downloads:   0.2069 × 300 = 62.07
  Webinar Attendance:  0.1724 × 80  = 13.79
  Demo Requests:       0.2586 × 75  = 19.40
                                     -------
  ACCOUNT SCORE:                     188.86

STEP 5: Identify Major Contributor
-----------------------------------
Highest contribution: Content Downloads (62.07 points)

STEP 6: Classify into Cohort
-----------------------------
Account Score = 188.86
Range: 100-199 → COHORT 1

STEP 7: User Commits to Cohorts
--------------------------------
System counts all accounts:
  - Cohort 1: 45 accounts (including Acme Corp)
  - Cohort 2: 30 accounts
  - Cohort 3: 25 accounts

STEP 8: Calculate Cohort 1 Metrics
-----------------------------------
Account Count: 45

Win Rate:
  Base: 17.5%
  Volume Bonus: min(45 × 0.05, 5) = 2.25%
  Win Rate: 17.5% + 2.25% = 19.75%

Avg Deal Size:
  Base: $50,000
  Volume Adjustment: 1.0 (45 ≤ 100)
  Deal Size: $50,000

Sales Cycle:
  Base: 135 days
  Process Efficiency: 1.0 (45 ≤ 50)
  Cycle: 135 days

Forecasted Revenue:
  Accounts: 45
  Win Rate: 19.75% = 0.1975
  Deal Size: $50,000

---------------------------------

================================================================================
SUMMARY OF KEY FORMULAS
================================================================================

1. Weight% = (Proposed Score / Total Proposed Scores) × 100

2. Complete Score = Events × Proposed Score

3. Account Score = Σ[(Weight% / 100) × Complete Score]

4. Cohort Classification:
   - 100-199 → Cohort 1
   - 200-299 → Cohort 2
   - 300-400 → Cohort 3

5. Win Rate = Base Rate + min(Accounts × 0.05, 5)

6. Avg Deal Size = Base Size × (0.95 if Accounts>100, else 1.0)

7. Sales Cycle = Base Cycle × (0.9 if Accounts>50, else 1.0)

8. Revenue = Accounts × (Win Rate/100) × Deal Size × Attribution Factor

9. Delta% = ((Current - Previous) / Previous) × 100
